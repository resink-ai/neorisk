# crypto_exchange

## What this is

A spot crypto exchange with two order books, BTC-USD and ETH-USD: six market makers quote both sides around a drifting fair price, and retail traders send orders that often cross the spread (good-till-cancelled, immediate-or-cancel or fill-or-kill). Each book matches by price, then time; GTC remainders rest, IOC remainders are cancelled, FOK orders are filled in full or rejected, and some resting orders are cancelled by their trader hours later. The same event log is modeled three ways — a star schema with an accumulating snapshot of the order lifecycle, a Data Vault and one big table per grain — so one dataset tests a processor on three shapes of output.

Version `v1`, kind `synthetic`, from `examples/crypto_exchange` (profile `sample`, seed 7). The input is one ordered event log of 24,055 events; the output is 21 tables in three designs (`star`, `dv`, `obt`), and the answers to 3 questions.

## Raw data

Generated by the `datagen` pack [`examples/crypto_exchange/specs/pack.py`](../../examples/crypto_exchange/specs/pack.py); the business is described in its brief, [`examples/crypto_exchange/specs/brief.md`](../../examples/crypto_exchange/specs/brief.md). Licence: generated, no third-party data. Prices are integer ticks of $0.01 per lot, quantities integer lots, deposits ticks (USD) or lots (BTC, ETH); notional is price × qty in ticks; times are UTC.

## How the log was made

`uv run dataset build crypto_exchange` runs the pack with the `sample` profile (6 market makers and 60 retail traders over 5 days), seed 7, and no overlay, then writes the run as one log. Each entity row becomes an `<entity>_created` event at its creation time (no domain names); business events keep their columns. Every business event is keyed on the column a Kafka consumer would partition on (`[recipe.owners]` in `dataset.toml`): `symbol` for every order event (accepted, rejected, filled, cancelled) and every `trade`, because the book of that symbol is the shard that emits them and a consumer rebuilding a book needs an order's acceptance, its trades and its fill or cancellation in one ordered partition (a trade touches two traders' orders, so `trader_id` would split it); `trader_id` for `funds_deposited`.

The designs read the log alone. Simkit's own tables are used only to grade them (the `[[check]]` entries) and to answer the questions (`oracle.sql`).

Facts of this run the designs handle on purpose:

- `order_id`, `trader_id`, `buyer_id`, `seller_id`, `maker_order` and `taker_order` are plain integers in the pack, not references, and there is no orders entity: an order exists only through its events. Order ids are unique across both books.
- An order id appears in exactly one of `order_accepted` (9,578) and `order_rejected` (64: 46 FOK orders unfillable, 18 with insufficient balance). An accepted order ends in at most one of `order_filled` and `order_cancelled`.
- The log stops with orders still on the books: 1,030 GTC orders resting untouched and 17 resting after a partial fill. 25 user cancellations and 3 IOC cancellations came after a partial fill.
- A taker's `order_filled` and the fills of the makers it swept share one event time; the satellite and snapshot logic never relies on the order of events at one instant.
- Market makers are takers too (4,916 of 5,552 trades): as the fair price drifts, a new quote crosses another maker's old one.

## Designs

All three designs cover the same scope: traders; deposits; orders with their whole lifecycle (accepted or rejected, traded as maker or taker, filled, cancelled by the user or as an IOC remainder, or still resting at the end of the log); and trades, each with both of its orders and both of its traders. Nothing is left out: every event of the log is in every design. No entity attribute changes in the log, so no design keeps attribute history: the history here is the order's lifecycle, a sequence of events on one key. Symbol stays a column in every design, not a dimension or hub: simkit has no symbols table to check one against, and a symbol has no attribute beyond its name. The designs follow the patterns of the *Beyond Facts & Dimensions* atlas.

**`star` — star schema (Kimball).** A static `star_dim_traders` (segment, creation time) keyed on `trader_id` (no SCD2: nothing changes, so a surrogate key would add a lookup and buy nothing). One transaction fact per event, at the grain of the event: deposit, order acceptance, order rejection, trade, order fill and order cancellation. `star_fact_trades` carries both sides of the fill: maker and taker order ids, their traders (looked up from the acceptances), buyer and seller, the taker's side, price, qty and notional. `star_fact_order_lifecycle` is the accumulating snapshot: one row per order id, accepted or rejected, with its terms (trader, symbol, side, type, price, qty), its milestone times (`submitted_at`, `accepted_at` or `rejected_at`, first and last trade, `filled_at`, `cancelled_at`), the trade counts as maker and taker, the filled quantity and notional, the quantity cancelled or still resting, and `outcome`: `rejected`, `filled`, `cancelled_user` or `cancelled_ioc` (whatever was filled before), else at the end of the log `partial_resting` if anything traded and `resting` if nothing did. The atlas's verdict holds: the snapshot answers "how did each order end" with one scan, at the cost of a row that is rewritten at every fill in a live system and of an outcome rule that lives in the build. Lifecycle facts are exempt from `[[check]]`; every other star table is checked against simkit. No calendar dimension (a date dimension cannot be checked against a simkit table).

**`dv` — Data Vault 2.0.** Hubs for traders and orders (`<e>_hk = md5(key as text)`; an order's business key is `order_id`, first seen at its acceptance or rejection, whose event type is the hub row's `record_source`). Links for order ownership (order, trader), the trade (maker order, taker order, buyer, seller, with `trade_id` as dependent child key, since the same two orders can in principle trade more than once) and the deposit (trader, with the event id as dependent child key). Satellites: the trader's `profile`; on the order hub, `details` (the terms as submitted) and `status`, one row per state of the order (open at acceptance or rejected with its reason, then filled, or cancelled with its reason and the quantity left); and `details` satellites on the trade and deposit links. Every satellite row has `hashdiff` = md5 of its attributes joined by `'||'` (nulls as `''`) and is written only when the hashdiff differs from the parent's previous row. `load_dts` is the event time, `record_source` the log event type. As the atlas says, a new event type is a new satellite and nothing is remodeled, and every load is insert-only and auditable; the price is that neither an order's outcome nor its filled quantity is stored anywhere: every question re-derives them from the status satellite and the trade link, and a trader's role in a trade takes two hops (trade link → order → order-trader link).

**`obt` — one big table.** One wide row per fact grain, every attribute bound as of the fact's time (here the creation value, since nothing changes). `obt_trades` is one row per trade with the UTC trade date, notional, the maker's and the taker's order terms, trader ids and segments, and the buyer's and seller's segments. `obt_orders` is one row per order id with the trader's segment and the lifecycle flattened into the same milestone, count, quantity and `outcome` columns as the star's snapshot. `obt_deposits` is one row per deposit with the trader's segment. The atlas's trade-off shows: the trade row is the shape a market-surveillance model reads and every question is one `GROUP BY`, but each grain repeats its dimensions (a trade row carries four trader segments), and an order's filled quantity in `obt_orders` restates what `obt_trades` already holds.

## Questions

- **`q1_volume_by_symbol_day`** — per symbol and UTC day: trades, quantity in lots and notional (price × qty, in ticks). A plain rollup of the trade fact; it checks that every design keeps every trade, its price and quantity exactly, and that notional stays a `BIGINT` (the largest trade is about 2.4 × 10⁷ ticks, a day's BTC notional about 5.5 × 10⁹). Star and OBT sum the stored notional, the vault computes it from the trade satellite.
- **`q2_maker_taker_by_segment`** — per trader segment: trades and quantity on the maker (resting) side and on the taker (incoming) side. Needs both sides of each trade bound to a trader: the oracle derives them from buyer, seller and the taker's side; the star reads the maker and taker traders off the trade fact, the vault walks from the trade link through each order hub to the order-trader link, the OBT reads the segments off the trade row.
- **`q3_order_outcomes`** — orders by type and final outcome (`filled`, `cancelled_user`, `cancelled_ioc`, `rejected`, `resting`, `partial_resting`), with their ordered and filled quantity. Needs the lifecycle: an order's outcome is its terminal event if any, and for an order still on the book at the end of the log, whether it traded; filled quantity counts the order's trades on either side, as taker on entry and as maker while it rests. Star and OBT read `outcome` and `filled_qty` from one row per order; the vault derives them from the status satellite and the trade link.

## Extend

- **More rows:** run the pack with a bigger profile or `--set` (e.g. `uv run datagen run examples/crypto_exchange --profile sample --set traders=500`) and make it a new dataset or version with that recipe in `dataset.toml`; every design and question is written against the log and keeps working.
- **An overlay:** the pack has none yet; an overlay (say, a spoofing or wash-trading ring with labels) would be a new dataset, e.g. `datasets/crypto_exchange-<overlay>/` with `overlay = ["<overlay>"]`; it adds labels and new actors, so owners and checks may need entries.
- **A new design:** add its id to `designs` (three per dataset), write `specs/<design>.sql` with `<design>_`-prefixed `-- table:` blocks over the log views, add `[[check]]`s, and an answer file in every question folder.
- **A new question:** a folder under `specs/questions/<id>/` with `oracle.sql` (reads `sim.<table>` only) and one `<design>.sql` per design (reads that design's tables only); all answers must have the same columns and agree with the oracle.
- **A new engine port:** write `engines/<engine>/` from `specs/<design>.sql`. It must read only the log and match `results/` exactly.

Any change to `specs/`, `dataset.toml` or the pack changes the build. Once a version is recorded in `v1.lock.json`, changed output needs a new version (`v2`).
