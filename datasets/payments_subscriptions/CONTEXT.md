# payments_subscriptions

## What this is

A streaming service that sells monthly plans (basic $8, standard $13, premium $20): every subscriber starts a 14-day free trial, some cancel during it, the rest are charged when it ends and every 30 days after; a declined charge enters dunning, with retries 3, 5 and 7 days after the failure before an involuntary cancel, and some subscribers cancel at a renewal. The same event log is modeled three ways — a star schema with an accumulating snapshot of the subscription lifecycle, an activity schema and an anchor model — so one dataset tests a processor on three shapes of output.

Version `v1`, kind `synthetic`, from `examples/payments_subscriptions` (profile `sample`, seed 7). The input is one ordered event log of 2,452 events; the output is 30 tables in three designs (`star`, `act`, `an`), and the answers to 3 questions.

## Raw data

Generated by the `datagen` pack [`examples/payments_subscriptions/specs/pack.py`](../../examples/payments_subscriptions/specs/pack.py); the business is described in its brief, [`examples/payments_subscriptions/specs/brief.md`](../../examples/payments_subscriptions/specs/brief.md). Licence: generated, no third-party data. Money is integer cents; times are UTC.

## How the log was made

`uv run dataset build payments_subscriptions` runs the pack with the `sample` profile (500 subscribers over 90 days, all signed up in the first 45), seed 7, and no overlay, then writes the run as one log. Each entity row becomes an `<entity>_created` event at its creation time (no domain names); business events keep their columns. Every business event is keyed on `subscriber_id` (`[recipe.owners]` in `dataset.toml`): the subscriber is the pack's owner of the subscription machine, so a Kafka consumer partitioning on it sees each subscription's life in order. The pack has no `std` recipes, so there are no internal events.

The designs read the log alone. Simkit's own tables are used only to grade them (the `[[check]]` entries) and to answer the questions (`oracle.sql`).

Facts of this run the designs handle on purpose:

- A subscriber has exactly one subscription, started at the instant of the subscriber's creation with the subscriber's plan; no plan ever changes. The subscription is keyed on the `event_id` of its `subscription_started` where a design needs its own key (`subscription_id`).
- No subscription is cancelled involuntarily in this run: the 85 failed charges are 79 first attempts and 6 first retries, and every dunned subscriber either recovered on a retry or is still in dunning at the end of the log (5 are). All 156 cancellations are `trial_cancel` (124) or `voluntary` (32).
- The log stops mid-lifecycle: at its end 339 subscriptions are active (last event a paid invoice), 5 are in dunning and 156 are cancelled; none is still in trial.
- No two subscription events of one subscriber share an instant in this run. The pack can emit two at once (a final failed retry and the involuntary cancel), so every design breaks ties by log order (`seq`, or `activity_id` in the stream).

## Designs

All three designs cover the same scope: subscribers, and every event of their subscription (started, trial converted, invoice paid, charge failed, cancelled). Nothing is left out. No entity attribute changes in the log, so the history here is the subscription's lifecycle, a sequence of events on one key; the designs that keep a status derive it the same way, from the last event: `subscription_started` → `trial`, `trial_converted` → `converted` (the trial is over and the first charge is pending, for a few seconds), `invoice_paid` → `active`, `charge_failed` → `dunning`, `subscription_cancelled` → `cancelled`. The designs follow the patterns of the *Beyond Facts & Dimensions* atlas.

**`star` — star schema (Kimball).** A static dimension for subscribers, keyed on `subscriber_id` (no SCD2: plan and channel never change, so a surrogate key would add a lookup and buy nothing). One transaction fact per business event, at its grain: subscription start (with plan and price; `subscription_id` is its event id), trial conversion, paid invoice, failed charge and cancellation. `star_fact_subscription_lifecycle` is the accumulating snapshot: one row per subscription, with its milestone times (`started_at`, `converted_at`, first and last paid invoice, first and last failed charge, `cancelled_at`), the counts and amounts paid and failed, the last cycle paid, the cancellation's reason and cycles paid, and `final_status`, the status after the subscriber's last event (by event time, then log order). The atlas's verdict holds: the snapshot answers "where is each subscription now" with one scan, at the cost of a row that is rewritten at every invoice in a live system and of a status rule that lives in the build. Lifecycle facts are exempt from `[[check]]`; every other star table is checked against simkit. No `star_dim_date` (a date dimension cannot be checked against a simkit table).

**`act` — activity schema.** One stream, `act_subscriber_stream`, with one row per subscription event in the standard columns (`activity_id, ts, customer, activity, feature_1, feature_2, feature_3, revenue_impact, link, activity_occurrence, activity_repeated_at`), and the customer table `act_subscriber` (plan, signup channel, creation time). `customer` is the `subscriber_id`, `activity_id` the log's `event_id`, `ts` the event time. The activities and their text features:

| Activity | Event | `feature_1` | `feature_2` | `feature_3` | `revenue_impact` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `started_subscription` | `subscription_started` | plan | price (cents) | — | — |
| `converted_trial` | `trial_converted` | — | — | — | — |
| `paid_invoice` | `invoice_paid` | cycle | attempt | — | amount |
| `failed_charge` | `charge_failed` | cycle | attempt | reason | — |
| `cancelled_subscription` | `subscription_cancelled` | reason | cycles paid | — | — |

A failed charge's amount is not kept: it earns nothing, so it is not revenue, and it always equals the subscription's price (`feature_2` of `started_subscription`). `link` is null (no activity has a URL). `activity_occurrence` is the 1-based count of the customer's activities of that kind in `ts` order (ties by `activity_id`), and `activity_repeated_at` the `ts` of the customer's next one, else null. Each activity's keys (and its amount or reason where it is a plain column) are checked against its simkit event table. As the atlas says, one narrow table holds the whole journey and a new event is a new activity name, not a new table; the price is that typed attributes become text features to cast back, and every question is a window over the stream ("the customer's last activity", "the plan from their first one") instead of a join.

**`an` — anchor modeling.** Anchors for subscribers, subscriptions (`subscription_id` = the event id of `subscription_started`, with `started_at`), paid invoices and failed charges, each with its identity and time; one table per attribute; one tie per relation, named `an_<e1>_<role>_<e2>` (`an_subscriptions_heldby_subscribers`, `an_invoices_for_subscriptions`, `an_charge_failures_for_subscriptions`). Plan and price are static attributes of the subscription (the subscriber's plan is also kept, as the entity's own attribute); the subscription's status is historized, `an_subscriptions_status`, with `changed_at` and one row per change (same-instant events collapse to the last in log order; a repeated status, such as a second paid invoice, adds no row); the cancel reason and cycles paid are set once, at cancellation, and carry its `changed_at`. Nothing is null: a running subscription has no cancel-reason row. The atlas's trade-off shows: the status history is native and a new attribute is a new table, but the model is the widest (21 tables) and every question joins one table per attribute it touches.

## Questions

- **`q1_active_at_end_by_plan`** — subscribers whose subscription is active at the end of the log (their last event is a paid invoice), and the sum of their prices, by plan. Needs the lifecycle: a subscription's state is its last event, and 5 subscriptions that paid before are in dunning at the end and do not count. Star filters the snapshot's `final_status`; the stream takes each customer's last activity; the anchor model takes the latest status row.
- **`q2_paid_by_plan_cycle`** — invoices paid (count and amount) by the subscription's plan and the billing cycle. A plain fact-to-dimension rollup; it checks that every design keeps each paid invoice, including retries that recovered a cycle, with its cycle number and amount.
- **`q3_churn_by_reason`** — cancellations by reason and plan, with the mean cycles paid at cancellation (a float). It checks that every design keeps the cancellation's reason and its `cycles_paid`; the stream must cast its text features back to numbers.

## Extend

- **More rows:** run the pack with a bigger profile or `--set` (e.g. `uv run datagen run examples/payments_subscriptions --profile sample --set subscribers=2000`) and make it a new dataset or version with that recipe in `dataset.toml`; a bigger run is likely to produce involuntary cancellations, which every design already handles (the status and stream rules break same-instant ties by log order). Every design and question is written against the log and keeps working.
- **An overlay:** the pack has none; one would be a new dataset, e.g. `datasets/payments_subscriptions-<overlay>/` with `overlay = ["<overlay>"]`, and may need owners and checks for its new events.
- **A new design:** add its id to `designs` (three per dataset), write `specs/<design>.sql` with `<design>_`-prefixed `-- table:` blocks over the log views, add `[[check]]`s, and an answer file in every question folder.
- **A new question:** a folder under `specs/questions/<id>/` with `oracle.sql` (reads `sim.<table>` only) and one `<design>.sql` per design (reads that design's tables only); all answers must have the same columns and agree with the oracle.
- **A new engine port:** write `engines/<engine>/` from `specs/<design>.sql`. It must read only the log and match `results/` exactly.

Any change to `specs/`, `dataset.toml` or the pack changes the build. Once a version is recorded in `v1.lock.json`, changed output needs a new version (`v2`).
