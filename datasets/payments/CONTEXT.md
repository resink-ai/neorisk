# payments

## What this is

A card acquirer serving 120 merchants: customers pay by card, the issuer authorizes each payment, the merchant captures it about an hour later (sometimes retrying the capture with the same idempotency key) or the authorization expires after 7 days; captured payments may be partially refunded up to three times, some are disputed weeks later and a lost dispute charges them back; each merchant is paid out daily, net of refunds, chargebacks and fees, and customers pay their statement every 30 days. The same event log is modeled three ways — a star schema with an accumulating snapshot of the payment lifecycle, a Data Vault and one big table per grain — so one dataset tests a processor on three shapes of output.

Version `v1`, kind `synthetic`, from `examples/payments` (profile `sample`, seed 7). The input is one ordered event log of 28,917 events; the output is 40 tables in three designs (`star`, `dv`, `obt`), and the answers to 3 questions.

## Raw data

Generated by the `datagen` pack [`examples/payments/specs/pack.py`](../../examples/payments/specs/pack.py); the business is described in its brief, [`examples/payments/specs/brief.md`](../../examples/payments/specs/brief.md). Licence: generated, no third-party data. Money is integer cents; times are UTC.

## How the log was made

`uv run dataset build payments` runs the pack with the `sample` profile (400 customers over 45 days), seed 7, and no overlay, then writes the run as one log. Each entity row becomes an `<entity>_created` event at its creation time (no domain names); business events keep their columns. Every business event is keyed on the column a Kafka consumer would partition on (`[recipe.owners]` in `dataset.toml`): `payment_id` for every event of a payment's life (authorized, captured, replayed, expired, the three refund outcomes, dispute opened and resolved), because the payment is the pack's owner of that process; `customer_id` for `credit_line_opened`, `statement_paid` and `payment_declined` (an `over_limit` decline is refused before a payment exists, so its `payment_id` is null and cannot key it); `merchant_id` for `settlement_posted`. The `hold_*` events of the `std.holds` recipe keep the default owner, their partition.

The designs read the log alone. Simkit's own tables are used only to grade them (the `[[check]]` entries) and to answer the questions (`oracle.sql`).

Facts of this run the designs handle on purpose:

- 10 of the 109 declines are `over_limit`: refused on the customer's credit line before a payment row exists, so they have no `payment_id`. The other 99 (`risk`) have a payment row.
- The log stops mid-lifecycle: 62 authorized payments are neither captured nor expired yet, and 14 of the 16 disputes are still open (1 won, 1 lost).
- `credit_line_opened` is emitted at the instant of its customer's creation with `limit` equal to the customer's `credit_limit`, so it carries nothing the creation row does not.

## Engines

Ported to every engine: `engines/spark/{star,dv,obt}.sql` (Spark SQL) and `engines/flink/{star,dv,obt}.sql` (Flink SQL, run by both `flink` and `flink-stream`). All 40 tables pass on each.

- **Both:** every join is `ON`, not `USING`: the views share `customer_id` and `merchant_id` across events. `count(*) FILTER (...)` is a `sum(CASE ...)` cast to BIGINT. Hashes need no NULL handling: every argument of `||` is already coalesced or a hash, and a BIGINT cast to text prints the same on every engine, so `md5`/`MD5` give DuckDB's lowercase hex.
- **Spark:** `QUALIFY lag(hashdiff) ... IS DISTINCT FROM hashdiff` is a filter over a subquery with `lag()`.
- **Flink:** the streaming planner rejects `LAG` ordered by `(load_dts, seq)`, so the three deduplicated satellites (`customers_statement`, `payments_replay`, `payments_dispute`) use a self-join instead. `ord` is `load_dts` and `seq` as sortable text, the previous row is the one at `MAX` of the earlier `ord` of the same parent, and a row is kept when that row's hashdiff differs or there is no earlier row. Both Flink modes give the same result. Text literals in `UNION ALL` branches are cast to STRING so that `'issued'` is not padded to `CHAR(7)`.
- **Batch Flink:** all 40 inserts run as one job. It once failed with "Insufficient number of network buffers" right after other jobs, and passed when run again.

```bash
uv run dataset run payments --engine spark          # flink, flink-stream: the cluster must be up
```

## Designs

All three designs cover the same scope: customers, cards and merchants; payments with their whole lifecycle (authorized, captured, capture replayed, expired, refund issued, failed or refused, dispute opened and resolved); declines (both reasons); merchant settlements; and customer statements. Left out of every design: the `hold_*` events of the `std.holds` recipe (internal credit and refund bookkeeping, fully determined by the payment and refund events), and `credit_line_opened`, which repeats the customer's creation row at the same instant. No entity attribute changes in the log, so no design keeps attribute history: the history here is the payment's lifecycle, a sequence of events on one key. The designs follow the patterns of the *Beyond Facts & Dimensions* atlas.

**`star` — star schema (Kimball).** Static dimensions for customers, cards and merchants, keyed on their natural keys (no SCD2: nothing changes, so a surrogate key would add a lookup and buy nothing). One transaction fact per event group, at the grain of the event: authorization, decline, capture, capture replay, expiration, refund decision (`outcome` issued, failed or refused), dispute opened, dispute resolved, settlement and statement. `star_fact_declines` carries the card of the declining customer (one card per customer), so an `over_limit` decline without a payment still has a brand. `star_fact_payment_lifecycle` is the accumulating snapshot: one row per payment, with its milestone times (`authorized_at`, `declined_at`, `captured_at`, `expired_at`, first and last refund, `disputed_at`, `dispute_resolved_at`), the amounts captured, refunded and charged back, the counts of replays and refund outcomes, and `final_status` by precedence: `declined`, else `charged_back` (a lost dispute) > `refunded` (any refund issued) > `captured` > `expired` > `authorized` (and `pending` if the issuer has not answered, which no payment of this run is). The atlas's verdict holds: the snapshot answers "where did each payment end up" with one scan, at the cost of a row that is rewritten at every milestone in a live system and of a status rule that lives in the build. Lifecycle facts are exempt from `[[check]]`; every other star table is checked against simkit. No `star_dim_date` (a date dimension cannot be checked against a simkit table).

**`dv` — Data Vault 2.0.** Hubs for customers, cards, merchants and payments (`<e>_hk = md5(key as text)`); links for card ownership (card, customer), the payment (payment, customer, card, merchant), and three links with the event id as dependent child key because one parent has many of them: decline (customer, merchant, `decline_id`), refund (payment, `refund_id`) and settlement (merchant, `settlement_id`). Satellites hold the attributes and, on the payment hub, one satellite per event group of its life: `details` (amount at creation), `authorization`, `capture`, `replay`, `expiry` and `dispute` (one row per state: open with its reason, then resolved with the reason carried, the outcome and the chargeback); declines, refunds and settlements have their own satellites on their links, customers a `statement` satellite. Every satellite row has `hashdiff` = md5 of its attributes joined by `'||'` (nulls as `''`) and is written only when the hashdiff differs from the parent's previous row. A decline's `payment_id` is an attribute of its satellite, not a link key, because the `over_limit` ones have none. `load_dts` is the event time, `record_source` the log event type. As the atlas says, a new event type is a new satellite and nothing is remodeled, and every load is insert-only and auditable; the price is that "final status" is not stored anywhere and every question re-derives it from six satellites.

**`obt` — one big table.** One wide row per fact grain, every attribute bound as of the fact's time (here the creation value, since nothing changes). `obt_payments` is one row per payment with the customer's segment, country and credit limit, the card's brand, the merchant's MCC and country, and the lifecycle flattened into the same milestone and amount columns as the star's snapshot plus `final_status`. Declines are their own grain, `obt_declines` (one row per `payment_declined`, with the same attributes): an `over_limit` decline has no payment row, so a single `obt_payments` would need rows with a null key; a `risk` decline appears in both tables, as a declined payment and as a decline. `obt_settlements` (with the merchant's attributes and the UTC posting date) and `obt_statements` (with the customer's and card's) complete the scope. The atlas's trade-off shows: the payment row is the ML-feature shape and every question is one `GROUP BY`, but each grain repeats its dimensions and a question that crosses grains (declined attempts next to payments) needs two tables.

## Questions

- **`q1_money_by_mcc`** — per merchant MCC: payments authorized, and the amounts captured, refunded (refunds issued) and charged back. Needs the lifecycle's money events across four event types without double counting; the row set is every MCC with a payment. Star sums the transaction facts; the vault sums the satellites through the payment link; the OBT sums the flattened columns.
- **`q2_final_status_by_brand`** — payments by final status and card brand, with the precedence charged_back > refunded > captured > expired > authorized, and declines counted separately (one per `payment_declined`, both reasons, the brand of the customer's card). Needs the lifecycle: a payment's status is its last milestone by precedence, not any single event; 62 payments are still only authorized and 14 disputes are still open at the end of the log. Star reads `final_status` from the snapshot, the OBT from `obt_payments`, the vault derives it from its satellites.
- **`q3_settlement_by_country`** — settlements posted and their net payout per merchant country and UTC day. A plain fact-to-dimension rollup over the merchant's daily payout; it checks that every design keeps settlements and their amounts exactly.

## Extend

- **More rows:** run the pack with a bigger profile or `--set` (e.g. `uv run datagen run examples/payments --profile sample --set customers=2000`) and make it a new dataset or version with that recipe in `dataset.toml`; every design and question is written against the log and keeps working.
- **An overlay:** `card_testing` (stolen cards tested with bursts of tiny authorizations at one weak merchant, then used once for a large purchase) as a new dataset, e.g. `datasets/payments-card_testing/` with `overlay = ["card_testing"]`; it adds labels and new actors, so owners and checks may need entries.
- **A new design:** add its id to `designs` (three per dataset), write `specs/<design>.sql` with `<design>_`-prefixed `-- table:` blocks over the log views, add `[[check]]`s, and an answer file in every question folder.
- **A new question:** a folder under `specs/questions/<id>/` with `oracle.sql` (reads `sim.<table>` only) and one `<design>.sql` per design (reads that design's tables only); all answers must have the same columns and agree with the oracle.
- **A new engine port:** write `engines/<engine>/` from `specs/<design>.sql`. It must read only the log and match `results/` exactly.

Any change to `specs/`, `dataset.toml` or the pack changes the build. Once a version is recorded in `v1.lock.json`, changed output needs a new version (`v2`).
