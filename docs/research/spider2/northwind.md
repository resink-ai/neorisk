# Northwind (Spider 2.0-lite): reverse-engineered event log

Source: `data/spider2/northwind.sqlite` (240 KB), opened read-only. `scripts/northwind/rq.py` copies the file into an in-memory SQLite and runs named blocks; the file itself is never written.

Every number below comes from one of these scripts:
- a named query in `scripts/northwind/q*.sql`;
- `f1_float4.py` (Python, float4 checks);
- `f2_duck_float4.py` (DuckDB, the views engine).

The raw outputs are in `scripts/northwind/out_*.txt`. A reference such as `[q2:shipped_vs_required]` means file `q2_keys_lifecycle.sql`, block `-- name: shipped_vs_required`. Gold answer CSVs are in `scripts/northwind/gold/`.

## 0. What this catalog is

- **Dataset.** This is Microsoft's **Northwind Traders** sample, the original 1996–1998 edition (830 orders, 2,155 lines, 77 products, 91 customers). It is not the 2006+ Access "Northwind 2007" redesign.
- **Exact lineage.** It comes from the PostgreSQL port **`pthom/northwind_psql`** (`northwind.sql`), loaded into SQLite with underscores removed from names (`order_details` keeps its underscore; `us_states` → `usstates`, `employee_territories` → `employeeterritories`). The evidence:
  - pthom's port adds **`us_states`** (51 rows, with `stateregion` east/midwest/north/south/west) and **shippers 4–6** (Alliance Shippers, UPS, DHL), which are not in Microsoft's script. Both are here [q1:row_counts, q3:usstates_regions].
  - pthom declares `freight`, `unit_price` and `discount` as PostgreSQL `real` (float4) and dumps them with 9 significant digits. Its first order literally reads `… 3, 32.3800011, 'Vins et alcools Chevalier' …`, which is the value stored here (§3.1).
  - pthom's pictures and photos are the literal `'\x'` (an empty bytea). Here they are TEXT `\x` (2 bytes) in all 8 categories and all 9 employees, despite the `BLOB` declaration [q1:typeof_columns, q1:blobs].
  - Original Microsoft typos survive: `Blondesddsl père et fils`, `Wolski  Zajazd` (two spaces), territory `Georgetow`, photo path `http://accweb/emmployees/…` [q2:ship_address_differences, q1:photopath_typo].
- **Spider additions.** **`customergroupthreshold`** (4 rows: Low 0–999.9999, Medium 1000–4999.9999, High 5000–9999.9999, Very High 10000–922337203685477.6) is not in pthom or Microsoft [q3:thresholds]. The top value is SQL Server's `money` maximum (922,337,203,685,477.5807), so the table was made on SQL Server. It looks like the `CustomerGroupThresholds` table from the *SQL Practice Problems* book's Northwind; that is **unverified**.
- **Empty tables.** `customercustomerdemo` and `customerdemographics` have 0 rows, as in the original.
- **Row counts** [q1:row_counts]: orders 830, order_details 2,155, products 77, customers 91, suppliers 29, employees 9, employeeterritories 49, territories 53, region 4, shippers 6, categories 8, usstates 51, customergroupthreshold 4, plus the 2 empty tables. Total 3,366.
- **Licence.** Microsoft publishes the Northwind script in `microsoft/sql-server-samples` under the **MIT licence**. pthom's repository `LICENSE` reproduces the **Microsoft Public License (Ms-PL)** from the old CodePlex page. The origin of the thresholds table is unknown.
- **Nature.** Fictitious company **course and sample data**, hand-made in the 1990s. It is not real and not generated by a simulator.

## 2. Event catalog

**Order lifecycle.**
- `orderdate`, `requireddate` and `shippeddate` are all `YYYY-MM-DD` text [q1:date_formats].
- `requireddate` is a **promise set at placement**: required − order is exactly 28 days for 701 orders, 14 for 68 and 42 for 61 [q2:required_minus_order]. 89 customers have a 28-day order, and 43 each have a 14- or 42-day one, so the lead is not a per-customer constant [q3:required_lead_customers].
- `shippeddate` is the only later state change. There is no delivered date, no cancel and no status column.
- Shipment always follows the order, by 1–37 days, so never on the same day; 0 orders were shipped before being ordered [q2:shipped_minus_order, q2:shipped_before_order].
- `shipvia` and `freight` are present on all 830 orders, including the 21 unshipped ones, so both are fixed at placement [q2:unshipped_orders].

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|

**Total events: 3,794.**

Entities (11 tables, 381 rows): categories 8, suppliers 29, products 77, shippers 6, region 4, territories 53, employees 9 (created_at = hiredate), employeeterritories 49 (link entity, ext_id = territoryid), customers 91, usstates 51, customergroupthreshold 4.

What is **not** an event, and why:
- **Product stock** (`unitsinstock`, `unitsonorder`, `reorderlevel`, `discontinued`) is a snapshot that orders do not explain: there is no inventory ledger and no purchase orders. It is entity state at N.
- **Product list price.** It changed during the window (§4), but the change is not recorded. The entity holds the final list price, and the price paid lives on E2.
- **Hire and birth dates** are 1992–1994 and 1937–1966, years before the first order (1996-07-04) [q3:employees_hire_vs_first_order]. They are entity attributes, with hiredate as `created_at`.
- **The two empty tables** have no entity; their views return 0 typed rows.
- **customergroupthreshold and usstates** are reference data with no time.

Snapshot **N = 1998-05-06**: the last orderdate and the last shippeddate [q2:last_dates].

## 3. Rebuild rules and blockers to an exact round trip

1. **The float4 artefact in every REAL money and discount column.**
   - `freight`, `order_details.unitprice`, `order_details.discount` and `products.unitprice` hold float64 values parsed from a float4 printed at 9 significant digits. For example, 32.38 is stored as 32.3800011, 9.80 as 9.80000019 and 0.05 as 0.0500000007 [q3:discount_values, f1].
   - Only 38 of 830 freights, 1,286 of 2,155 line prices, 1,471 of 2,155 discounts and 60 of 77 list prices are the plain decimal double (they are float4-exact, like 14.0, 0.25 or 81.75).
   - **But every value rebuilds from integer cents:** `float(printf('%.9g', float32(cents/100)))` equals the stored value in 830 of 830, 2,155 of 2,155, 2,155 of 2,155 and 77 of 77 [f1]. In DuckDB, `CAST(printf('%.9g', CAST(c/100.0 AS FLOAT)) AS DOUBLE)` matches the same 830, 2,155, 2,155 and 77, while plain `CAST(… AS FLOAT)::DOUBLE` widening matches only 31 freights [f2].
   - **Recommendation:** carry money as `amount()` in cents and discount as an integer percent, and apply this rule in the views. That way stage 2 can generate clean cents. Carrying `f64` verbatim is the fallback, and it is trivially exact.
2. **There is no order total.** Northwind stores no order or invoice total, so there is nothing to reconcile.
   - The line net `unitprice·quantity·(1−discount)` lands on whole cents in only 870 of 2,155 lines as stored floats. With exact cents and percent it lands on whole cents in 2,074 of 2,155 [f1]; the remaining lines have sub-cent net values.
   - Analyst queries that sum net amounts (no Spider question here) get float noise. Compare those with the 1e-9 tolerance.
3. **The thresholds table** must be carried as `f64` verbatim. `rangetop` = 999.9999, 4999.9999, 9999.999900000001 and 922337203685477.6 is neither float4-derived nor on the cent grid [f1, q3:thresholds]. `rangebottom` is INTEGER [q1:typeof_columns].
4. **Ship address is order data, not customer data.** The six ship columns equal the customer's (NULL-safe) in only 748 of 830 orders [q2:ship_address_vs_customer]. The differences involve 10 customers:
   - spelling variants in 5: ALFKI 5 orders (`Alfred's`), BLONP 11, GALED 5, TRADH 6, WOLZA 7;
   - a different street in 5: AROUT 13, CHOPS 8, LAUGB 3, RICSU 10, WHITC 14 [q2:ship_address_differences].
   - ALFKI uses two ship names [q3:multi_address_customer].

   So all six columns ride on E1 verbatim.
5. **NULL vs empty.** No `''` exists in any column [q1:null_vs_empty]. NULLs:
   - orders: shipregion 507, shippostalcode 19, shippeddate 21;
   - customers: region 60, fax 22, postalcode 1;
   - suppliers: region 20, fax 16, homepage 24;
   - employees: region 4, reportsto 1.
6. **Blobs are text.** `categories.picture` and `employees.photo` are TEXT `\x` in every row. They must be written back as TEXT, not as a BLOB, or the INTEGER/REAL/text comparison fails on type.
7. **Text.** `territorydescription` has no trailing padding (it was trimmed, unlike the nchar original) [q1:whitespace]. `employees.notes` reaches 448 characters. Keep both byte-exact. `territoryid` is text with leading zeros.
8. **Dates.** All dates are `YYYY-MM-DD`. Read them as UTC midnight and format them back with `%Y-%m-%d`. `requireddate` can be carried as text or as lead days (14/28/42 exactly, 830 of 830), and both rebuild exactly. The 21 unshipped orders rebuild as NULL simply because they have no E3.
9. **Rows without time.** Only entities lack time. No invented times are needed; customers' `created_at` is the first order by rule, and 2 customers without orders get N by rule.

**Verdict:** exact with caveats. The one real decision is how to rebuild the float4 artefact; the cents rule is proven exact in both Python and DuckDB.

## 4. Measured quirks

- **Order / required / shipped lifecycle** [q2:shipped_vs_required]:

  | outcome | orders |
  |---|---:|
  | shipped before the required date | 769 |
  | shipped on the required date | 3 |
  | shipped after the required date | 37 |
  | not shipped by N | 21 |

  - Late by Spider's definition (≥ required) is 40 of 809 shipped, 4.9%.
  - The 21 unshipped orders are all the most recent: ordered 1998-04-08..05-06, with required dates 1998-05-06..06-11. They are open at N, not lost [q2:unshipped_orders].
  - The shipping lag does not drift by year: mean 8.11 d (1996), 8.91 (1997), 8.04 (1998) [q3:ship_lag_by_year].
  - Shipping lag by carrier, as mean days and late count: Speedy 8.57 / 12, United 9.23 / 19, Federal 7.47 / 9 [q3:ship_lag_by_shipper].
- **Business days only.** Orders, required dates and shipments all fall Mon–Fri, with 0 on weekends [q2:weekdays]. The gap between consecutive orderids is 0, 1 or 3 days, nothing else (350, 383, 96) [q2:business_days_gaps]. This is the hand-made sample's "one business day at a time" rhythm.
- **orderid is in date order** (829 of 829 non-decreasing pairs) [q2:id_vs_orderdate].
- **Two price regimes.**
  - 75 of 77 products are sold at two prices, and 2 at three (products 11 and 42) [q3:prices_per_product, q3:three_price_products].
  - The old price is about 0.8 × the current list price: ratio 0.8 on 499 lines, 0.792–0.799 on most of the rest [q3:other_price_ratio].
  - The switch is per product. The last old-price line is order 10657 (1997-09-04); the first new-price lines run from 1996-07-04 to 1997-11-25 [q3:price_change_date, q3:price_switch_by_orderid].
  - Only 1 product sells at an old price after its first list-price sale [q3:price_regime_by_orderid].
- **Discounts** take 11 values: 0 (1,317), 5%, 10%, 15%, 20%, 25% (154–185 each), plus 1–4% and 6% on 1–3 lines [q3:discount_values]. 667 of 830 orders use a single discount on all lines [q3:discount_by_customer_order].
- **Order 11077** (RATTC, 1998-05-06, the last day) has **25 lines**. Every other order has 1–6 [q3:lines_per_order, q3:order_25_lines].
- **Volume growth.** About 22–33 orders per month in 1996, 29–48 in 1997 and 54–74 in early 1998. 1998-05 has 14, because it is truncated at N [q2:orders_per_month].
- **Unused or orphan-ish rows.** Shippers 4–6 are never used, 2 customers have no orders, 4 territories have no employee, and both demographics tables are empty.
- **Stock snapshot.** 10 products are discontinued, yet all 10 were still ordered in 1998 (the last on 1998-05-06). 5 are out of stock, 17 have units on order, and 2 are below their reorder level [q3:products_stock, q3:discontinued_last_order].

## 6. simkit mapping sketch

```python

p.entity("suppliers", key="supplier_id", cols={"ext_id": text(), "companyname": text(), ..., "homepage": opt(text())})
p.entity("products", key="product_id", cols={"ext_id": text(), "productname": text(), "supplier_id": ref("suppliers"),
         "category_id": ref("categories"), "quantityperunit": opt(text()), "unitprice": amount("USD"),
         "unitsinstock": i64(), "unitsonorder": i64(), "reorderlevel": i64(), "discontinued": i64()})
p.entity("shippers", ...); p.entity("region", ...); p.entity("territories", ... "region_id": ref("region"))
p.entity("employees", key="employee_id", cols={..., "reportsto": opt(ref("employees")), "photo": opt(text()), "notes": opt(text())})
p.entity("employeeterritories", key="et_id", cols={"ext_id": text(), "employee_id": ref("employees"), "territory_id": ref("territories")})
p.entity("customers", key="customer_id", cols={"ext_id": text(), "companyname": text(), ...})
p.entity("usstates", ...); p.entity("customergroupthreshold", key="group_id", cols={"ext_id": text(), "rangebottom": i64(), "rangetop": f64()})
p.event("order_placed", cols={"customer_id": ref("customers"), "orderid": i64(), "employee_id": ref("employees"),
        "required_lead_days": cat("14", "28", "42"), "ship_via": ref("shippers"), "freight": amount("USD"),
        "ship_name": text(), "ship_address": text(), "ship_city": text(), "ship_region": opt(text()),
        "ship_postal_code": opt(text()), "ship_country": text()})
p.event("order_line_added", cols={"customer_id": ref("customers"), "orderid": i64(), "product_id": ref("products"),
        "unit_price": amount("USD"), "quantity": i64(), "discount_pct": i64()})
p.event("order_shipped", cols={"customer_id": ref("customers"), "orderid": i64()})
```

Ingest:
- `order_placed owner=customer_id ord=orderid`, ORDER BY orderid.
- `order_line_added owner=customer_id ord=orderid`, ORDER BY orderid, productid.
- `order_shipped owner=customer_id ord=orderid`, ORDER BY orderid.
- Placement sorts before its lines because its block comes first; shipment is always on a later day.
- Money in = `CAST(round(x*100) AS INTEGER)`; discount = `round(discount*100)`.

Views:
- money out = `CAST(printf('%.9g', CAST(c/100.0 AS FLOAT)) AS DOUBLE)`.
- `orders.shippeddate` = LEFT JOIN E3.
- `requireddate` = orderdate + lead days.
- The 2 empty tables are `SELECT … WHERE false`.

Stage-2 owners:
- **customer** (91, each placing orders on business days, with an orders-per-customer weight);
- **employee** as the sales rep (an attribute draw, or a shard if workload matters);
- **shipper shard** (3 active), which draws the ship lag (lognormal, about 8 d) and emits E3 on a weekday, with the lateness rule against the 14/28/42-day promise.

There is no request/reply need beyond placement → shipment. The volume is about 3.8k events, so no sample profile is needed.

## 7. Open questions and risks

- **The customergroupthreshold origin** (*SQL Practice Problems*?) is unverified, and its licence is unknown. It is 4 rows of reference data, so the risk is low.
- **Licence wording conflicts.** Microsoft's sql-server-samples is MIT; pthom quotes Ms-PL from CodePlex. Both are permissive with attribution. Confirm before redistributing rows; fitting statistics is fine.
- **Float4 rule.** It is exact today in Python and DuckDB [f1, f2], but it depends on `printf('%.9g')` behaving the same across DuckDB versions. Add a unit test, or fall back to `f64` verbatim.
- **No cancel or delivery data.** Unshipped orders are only "open at N". A sim cannot learn cancellation, and should not invent it.
- **Price-regime change times are unobserved.** For stage 2, model them as a per-product switch date (×1.25 from the old to the list price) drawn between 1996-07 and 1997-11. Product 11's and product 42's third price shows at least one extra step.
- **Business-day calendar and order 11077's 25 lines** are hand-made features. Reproduce them deliberately or record them as deviations.
